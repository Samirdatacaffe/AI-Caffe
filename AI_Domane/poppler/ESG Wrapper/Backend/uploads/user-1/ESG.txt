  SECTION 1 — ESG Assurance: Foundations & Context

1.1 What Is ESG Assurance?

ESG assurance is an independent, structured process by which a qualified
third-party practitioner evaluates whether an organisation's
sustainability disclosures are accurate, complete, and fairly presented
in accordance with the applicable framework and criteria. It is distinct
from internal audit, management review, or data validation — it provides
external credibility to stakeholders including investors, regulators,
ratings agencies, and the public.

Assurance engagements on sustainability information are governed by one
or more professional standards that define the practitioner's
obligations, the acceptable evidence types, the form of the conclusion,
and the quality of documentation required.

1.2 Why Assurance Matters — India + Global Context

India — SEBI & BRSR Context

-   SEBI mandated BRSR (Business Responsibility & Sustainability
      Reporting) for the top 1,000 listed companies by market
      capitalisation from FY 2022-23.

-   BRSR Core — a subset of the most material KPIs — requires mandatory
      external assurance for the top 150 companies from FY 2023-24,
      scaling to top 1,000 over time.

-   As of 2025, fewer than ~25 SEBI-registered assurance providers exist
      against 1,800+ listed entities, creating significant capacity
      constraints.

-   SEBI has issued circulars requiring methodology disclosure,
      independence confirmations, and practitioner registration. ICAI
      has published a Technical Guide on ESG Assurance aligned with ISAE
      3000.

-   Regulators and investors expect a trajectory from limited assurance
      (early years) towards reasonable assurance on material KPIs over
      time.

Global Context

-   The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)
      mandates limited assurance immediately and reasonable assurance
      over time for ~50,000 companies.

-   The IAASB published ISSA 5000 (2024) as the first global standard
      written specifically for sustainability assurance.

-   CDP, GRI, ISSB/IFRS S1-S2 all reference external assurance as a
      quality signal. Many institutional investors require assured data
      before including it in portfolio scoring.

1.4 The Practitioner — Who Can Provide ESG Assurance?

India

-   Chartered Accountants registered with ICAI can provide ESG assurance
      under ISAE 3000 and the ICAI Technical Guide.

-   Cost Accountants (ICWA/ICMAI) may provide assurance on specific
      metrics (e.g., carbon accounting) where empanelled.

-   SEBI-registered assurance providers: a small group currently.
      Companies should verify current SEBI registration list.

-   Independence: The assurance provider must be independent of the
      client as defined by the relevant code of ethics (ICAI Code for
      CAs, IESBA Code for IAASB-aligned practitioners).

Global

-   Audit firms (Big 4 and mid-tier) providing assurance under ISAE
      3000, ISSA 5000, or AA1000AS.

-   Specialist sustainability assurance firms (e.g., Bureau Veritas,
      SGS, Intertek) typically provide verification under ISAE 3000 or
      ISO 14064-3.

-   Independence, objectivity, and professional competence are universal
      requirements across all standards.

  SECTION 2 — Assurance Standards: Complete Reference

2.1 ISAE 3000 (Revised) — The Primary Standard

Overview

Full name: International Standard on Assurance Engagements 3000
(Revised) — Assurance Engagements Other Than Audits or Reviews of
Historical Financial Information. Issued by the IAASB in 2013. This is
the most widely used standard for ESG/sustainability assurance globally
and is the primary reference for BRSR assurance in India.

Key Principles

-   Applies to both reasonable and limited assurance engagements.

-   Requires the practitioner to define: subject matter, suitable
      criteria, engagement objective, and the form of conclusion.

-   Three parties: the practitioner, the responsible party (company
      management), and the intended users.

-   Requires professional scepticism, professional judgement, and
      ethical requirements (independence, confidentiality, objectivity).

-   Documentation must be sufficient for an experienced practitioner
      with no prior knowledge to understand the work performed.

Subject Matter & Criteria

-   Subject matter: the ESG/sustainability disclosures being assured
      (e.g., GHG statement, BRSR Core KPIs, full sustainability report).

-   Criteria: the basis against which the subject matter is evaluated —
      must be suitable (relevant, complete, reliable, neutral,
      understandable). Examples: GHG Protocol, BRSR SEBI guidelines, GRI
      Standards, own disclosed methodology.

-   Criteria must be clearly described in the assurance report.

Engagement Acceptance & Continuance

-   Practitioner must assess: suitability of criteria, availability of
      evidence, capability and competence to perform the engagement.

-   Engagement letter must document: scope, level, fees,
      responsibilities, limitations, and intended users.

Risk Assessment — ISAE 3000

-   Identify and assess the risk of material misstatement of the subject
      matter information.

-   Inherent risk: susceptibility of a metric to misstatement absent
      controls (e.g., Scope 3 emissions have high inherent risk due to
      estimation uncertainty).

-   Control risk: the risk that controls do not prevent or detect
      misstatements.

-   Detection risk: the risk that procedures do not detect existing
      misstatements.

Evidence

-   Evidence must be sufficient (quantity) and appropriate (quality —
      relevance and reliability).

-   Evidence is more reliable when: obtained from external sources,
      generated by entity's own system with effective controls, in
      documentary form, original rather than copies.

-   Under limited assurance: primarily inquiry and analytical
      procedures. Under reasonable assurance: includes detailed tests of
      data, recalculations, reconciliations, confirmations.

Conclusion Wording — ISAE 3000

  📌 Limited Assurance: 'Based on our procedures and the evidence
  obtained, nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe
  that [subject matter] is not prepared, in all material respects, in
  accordance with [criteria].'

  📌 Reasonable Assurance: 'In our opinion, [subject matter] is
  prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with [criteria].'

Report Structure — ISAE 3000

-   Title (include 'Independent' to signal independence)

-   Addressee

-   Introduction: identification of subject matter, criteria,
      responsible party

-   Management's responsibility paragraph

-   Practitioner's responsibility and standard reference

-   Inherent limitations paragraph (especially relevant for
      forward-looking and Scope 3 information)

-   Summary of work performed (especially under limited assurance)

-   Conclusion paragraph

-   Signature, practitioner's address, date

  ⚠️ India: SEBI requires the assurance report to name the
  SEBI-registered assurance provider and include their registration
  number. Failure to include this can result in the assurance not being
  recognised.

2.2 ISAE 3410 — GHG Assurance

Overview

Full name: International Standard on Assurance Engagements 3410 —
Assurance Engagements on Greenhouse Gas Statements. Issued by IAASB.
This standard is purpose-built for standalone GHG verification
engagements, applying specifically when the subject matter is a GHG
statement.

Scope of Application

-   A GHG statement: a structured declaration of an entity's GHG
      emissions across Scope 1, Scope 2 (location-based or
      market-based), and Scope 3 categories.

-   Can be applied to: BRSR GHG metrics, CDP climate disclosure,
      science-based target baseline, standalone carbon inventory.

-   NOT designed for full ESG report assurance — use ISAE 3000 for that.

Key Technical Requirements

-   Criteria: GHG Protocol (Corporate Standard and Scope 2 Guidance) is
      the most widely accepted. Other acceptable criteria: ISO 14064-1,
      nationally mandated methodologies.

-   Emission factors: the practitioner must assess the appropriateness
      of emission factors used (e.g., IPCC, IEA, national grid factors,
      supplier-specific).

-   Organisational boundary: assess whether the entity's choice of
      boundary (equity share, financial control, operational control) is
      appropriate and consistently applied.

-   Base year: verify consistency of base year emissions and any
      restatements for acquisitions, disposals, or methodology changes.

-   Scope 3 categories: ISAE 3410 acknowledges high estimation
      uncertainty in Scope 3; inherent limitations paragraphs are almost
      always required.

Procedures Specific to ISAE 3410

-   Trace reported emissions to underlying activity data (fuel
      consumption records, utility bills, production volumes, travel
      logs, procurement data).

-   Recalculate emissions using stated emission factors. Identify any
      differences and assess materiality.

-   Verify emission factor sources and confirm they are current (IPCC AR
      factors updated, national grid factors for applicable year).

-   For market-based Scope 2: verify EAC (Energy Attribute Certificate)
      documentation, PPA details, supplier-specific emission factors.

-   For Scope 3: assess completeness of categories included, verify that
      material categories are not omitted, assess data quality from
      supply chain.

India-Specific Notes — ISAE 3410

-   Indian grid emission factors: use Central Electricity Authority
      (CEA) published grid emission factors for the relevant year. Do
      not use global averages.

-   BRSR requires disclosure of Scope 1, Scope 2, and (if available)
      Scope 3 total emissions. The practitioner must verify all in-scope
      categories.

-   GHG Protocol is accepted in India; ISO 14064-1 is also referenced by
      ICAI.

2.3 AA1000AS v3 — AccountAbility Assurance Standard

Overview

Published by AccountAbility. AA1000AS v3 is designed to assure an
organisation's adherence to the AA1000 AccountAbility Principles:
Inclusivity (stakeholder engagement), Materiality (what matters most),
Responsiveness (response to stakeholder concerns), and Impact
(measurement of outcomes).

Unique Features vs ISAE 3000

-   AA1000AS evaluates not just data accuracy but the QUALITY of the
      reporting process — how material topics were identified, how
      stakeholders were engaged, how responsive the report is to
      stakeholder concerns.

-   Less prescriptive on specific test procedures; more principle-based
      and qualitative.

-   Often used alongside GRI Standards reporting.

Three Types of Engagement Under AA1000AS

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Type                                Description

  Type 1                              Assurance provider evaluates
                                      adherence to AA1000 Principles
                                      only. Does not include data
                                      verification.

  Type 2                              Assurance provider evaluates
                                      adherence to AA1000 Principles AND
                                      verifies specific performance
                                      information (data).

  Specific subject matter             Only covers specified disclosures
                                      or topics, not the full report.
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

Key Procedures Under AA1000AS

-   Materiality assessment review: evaluate whether the entity's
      materiality process is robust, complete, and aligned with
      stakeholder expectations.

-   Stakeholder engagement review: assess the breadth, depth, and
      representativeness of stakeholder engagement. Review stakeholder
      mapping documents.

-   Completeness check: determine whether material issues are adequately
      disclosed and not omitted.

-   Responsiveness assessment: evaluate whether the report responds to
      stakeholder concerns raised during the period.

-   Data verification (Type 2 only): same evidence-based procedures as
      ISAE 3000 for specific KPIs.

Conclusion Wording — AA1000AS

  📌 The assurance provider concludes on the level of adherence to each
  of the four AA1000 Principles and (for Type 2) on the accuracy of
  specific data.

  📌 AA1000AS uses its own three-tier conclusion: High, Moderate, or Low
  level of adherence — distinct from limited/reasonable terminology.

2.4 ISSA 5000 — The New Global Standard

Overview

Full name: International Standard on Sustainability Assurance 5000.
Issued by the IAASB in 2024. ISSA 5000 is the first standard written
specifically and exclusively for sustainability assurance. It supersedes
ISAE 3000 as the preferred standard for comprehensive ESG assurance and
is designed to align with ISSB (IFRS S1/S2) and CSRD/ESRS requirements.

Key Differences from ISAE 3000

-   Written entirely in the sustainability assurance context — no need
      to 'adapt' a financial audit standard.

-   Includes specific requirements for: multi-stakeholder sustainability
      topics, complex value chain information, forward-looking claims,
      and scenario analysis.

-   More explicit guidance on: materiality for sustainability, double
      materiality (CSRD context), biodiversity, just transition, and
      human rights.

-   Scalable application: designed for both limited and reasonable
      assurance, with explicit scaling provisions.

-   Specific requirements for use of experts (e.g., GHG specialists,
      legal experts, biodiversity scientists) and internal audit.

Double Materiality — ISSA 5000 / CSRD Context

-   Financial materiality: information material to investors based on
      how sustainability risks/opportunities affect the company's
      financial performance.

-   Impact materiality: information material because of the company's
      actual or potential impacts on people and the environment.

-   CSRD/ESRS requires both. ISSA 5000 provides a framework for how the
      assurance practitioner assesses both dimensions.

Applicability in India

-   ISSA 5000 is not yet mandated in India as of 2025, but SEBI has
      signalled convergence with ISSB. Cross-listed Indian companies and
      those with EU nexus may adopt it voluntarily.

-   Indian CAs and SEBI-registered providers can use ISSA 5000 — ICAI
      has acknowledged it. It is expected to become the preferred
      standard in India within 2-4 years.

2.5 India National Standard — ICAI Technical Guide

Overview

The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) has published a
Technical Guide on ESG Assurance. While not a formal Engagement Standard
in the ISA sense, it provides authoritative guidance for ICAI members
providing ESG assurance in India. It draws on ISAE 3000 and ISAE 3410
and adapts them to the BRSR context.

Key Requirements Under ICAI Technical Guide

-   Independence: CAs must comply with the ICAI Code of Ethics. Existing
      statutory audit or tax client relationships do not automatically
      impair ESG assurance independence, but the practitioner must
      perform a fresh independence assessment.

-   Competence: The practitioner must have relevant knowledge of ESG
      reporting frameworks, GHG accounting, BRSR structure, and the
      industry of the client.

-   The guide endorses ISAE 3000 as the primary procedural framework
      with BRSR-specific adaptations.

-   BRSR Core KPIs: The guide identifies specific Key Performance
      Indicators that are subject to mandatory assurance (listed in
      Section 2.6 below).

SEBI Registration

-   Assurance providers for BRSR must be SEBI-registered (per SEBI
      circular on ESG rating and data providers).

-   The practitioner must include their SEBI registration number in the
      assurance report.

-   Only SEBI-registered entities can provide assurance that SEBI will
      recognise as satisfying the BRSR Core mandatory assurance
      requirement.

  ⚠️ As of 2025, only ~25 entities are SEBI-registered as assurance
  providers. The pool is expanding but remains severely constrained
  relative to demand.

  SECTION 3 — The 10-Step Engagement Programme: Full Procedures

This section provides the complete field-level content for each of the
10 steps in the ESGCaffe Assurance Wizard. For every step, it covers:
purpose, key decisions, procedures by assurance level, expected
evidence, common pitfalls, and India-specific notes.

Step 1 — Standard Selection & Scope

Purpose

Formally define which standard will govern the engagement, the exact
boundary of what is being assured, and the reporting frameworks the
assurance covers. This is foundational — all subsequent procedures,
evidence requirements, and conclusion wording flow from these decisions.

Key Decisions

-   Which standard? (See Section 2.) Default for India: ISAE 3000. For
      GHG-only: ISAE 3410.

-   Full report vs selected KPIs? BRSR Core mandates the latter for
      specific metrics. Full report assurance is optional but adds
      credibility.

-   Entity boundary: group vs parent vs BUs vs geographies. Must match
      the BRSR filing boundary.

-   Reporting frameworks covered: at minimum BRSR for Indian listed
      companies. May also include GRI, TCFD, CDP.

Procedure — Both Levels

-   Review the entity's BRSR filing and/or sustainability report to
      understand the full scope of disclosures.

-   Identify which KPIs are in BRSR Core (mandatory assurance) vs the
      broader BRSR (voluntary assurance).

-   Document the reporting period (financial year) and confirm it
      matches the period of the data to be assured.

-   Confirm the entity boundary with management and trace to the
      consolidation scope used in the financial statements.

-   Identify any entities or operations that are excluded from scope and
      document the reason.

Evidence

-   BRSR filing / sustainability report (final or near-final draft)

-   Entity boundary documentation (organisational chart, list of
      subsidiaries)

-   Prior year assurance report (if any) — to understand continuity
      decisions

India-Specific Notes

-   SEBI mandates that the scope of BRSR Core assurance must cover the
      same entities as the BRSR filing. You cannot narrow the entity
      boundary vs the BRSR filing without explicit disclosure.

-   If the company has operations in SEZs or JVs, confirm
      inclusion/exclusion is consistent with BRSR guidance.

  📌 ESGCaffe AI prompt guidance: 'What KPIs fall under BRSR Core
  mandatory assurance?' — answer: see Section 2.6 table.

Step 2 — Engagement Objective & Assurance Level

Purpose

Document the formal engagement objective, confirm the assurance level,
draft the intended conclusion wording, and identify all scope exclusions
that must be disclosed in the final report.

Assurance Level — Decision Framework

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Factor                              Indicators Favouring Each Level

  ESG data maturity                   Limited: first 1-3 years,
                                      spreadsheet-based / Reasonable:
                                      ESGCaffe or equivalent platform,
                                      strong controls

  BRSR Core requirement               Limited: acceptable initially /
                                      Reasonable: required trajectory for
                                      Top 1,000 over time

  Client risk appetite                Limited: lower risk of adverse
                                      conclusion / Reasonable: higher
                                      credibility with investors

  Assurance fee                       Limited: lower / Reasonable:
                                      significantly higher

  Timeframe                           Limited: faster (4-6 weeks) /
                                      Reasonable: longer (8-16 weeks)

  Controls environment                Limited: weak/developing controls
                                      acceptable / Reasonable: requires
                                      reliable controls to reduce
                                      substantive work
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

Engagement Letter — Required Elements

-   Identification of subject matter and criteria

-   Assurance level (limited or reasonable) and form of conclusion

-   Management's responsibilities (preparation of disclosures, provision
      of evidence, written representations)

-   Practitioner's responsibilities and scope of work

-   Fees and payment terms

-   Confidentiality provisions

-   Limitations on the face of the report

-   Statement that the report is for the benefit of identified intended
      users only

Scope Exclusions — Common Examples

-   Scope 3 categories not yet quantified (must disclose the excluded
      categories and reason)

-   Overseas subsidiaries below materiality threshold

-   Joint ventures where equity access to data is limited

-   Prior year comparatives (if only current year is being assured)

-   Forward-looking targets and commitments (assurance is typically on
      historical data only)

  ⚠️ Every exclusion MUST be disclosed in the assurance report.
  Undisclosed scope narrowing is an integrity risk.

Step 3 — Materiality & Risk Focus

Purpose

Establish the materiality threshold that will guide the depth of
procedures, identify the key ESG risk areas, and determine which sites,
processes, or data streams require heightened attention.

Materiality — Two Dimensions

-   Quantitative materiality: a numerical threshold below which
      misstatements are unlikely to affect users' decisions. Typical
      starting points: 5% of total GHG emissions for climate KPIs; 5% of
      total workforce for social KPIs; 5% of total energy or water
      consumption.

-   Qualitative materiality: factors that make a misstatement material
      regardless of size — regulatory significance, reputational
      exposure, first-year disclosures, analyst/investor focus,
      high-profile commitments (net zero, gender parity targets).

-   Performance materiality: set below overall materiality (typically
      60-80%) to provide a buffer for aggregated misstatements.

Risk Assessment — Framework

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Risk Factor                         Assessment Approach

  Inherent risk                       How susceptible is this metric to
                                      misstatement absent any controls?
                                      High for: Scope 3, estimation-based
                                      metrics, first-year disclosures,
                                      complex calculations.

  Control risk                        How likely is it that controls fail
                                      to prevent/detect misstatements?
                                      High for: manual spreadsheet-based
                                      processes, single point of entry,
                                      no independent review.

  Information risk                    Complexity of data collection
                                      (e.g., multi-site, multi-system,
                                      third-party data). High for: supply
                                      chain data, satellite/IoT sensors,
                                      contractor-collected data.

  Regulatory risk                     Metrics specifically called out by
                                      SEBI or investors as high-scrutiny.
                                      GHG emissions, LTIFR, board
                                      diversity are typically high.
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

High-Risk Indicators

-   First year of assurance on a metric

-   Prior year restatements or qualifications

-   Material acquisitions or disposals during the year

-   Changes in reporting methodology or emission factors

-   Weak or newly implemented internal controls

-   Significant operational changes (new facilities, new products)

-   Metrics close to a disclosed target or threshold

India-Specific Risk Themes

-   GHG: Scope 2 grid emission factor selection (India CEA vs IEA vs
      outdated values)

-   Water: incorrect identification of water-stressed areas (use WRI
      Aqueduct for India)

-   H&S: under-reporting of incidents in manufacturing/construction
      sectors

-   Governance: discrepancies between Board minutes and disclosed ESG
      committee activities

Step 4 — Evidence Types & Data Sources

Purpose

Identify and assess the acceptability of evidence types, map primary
data systems, and determine the approach to walkthroughs and site
visits.

Evidence Hierarchy — Reliability Ranking

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Evidence Type                       Reliability Level & Notes

  External confirmations (utilities,  High — third-party source,
  logistics)                          independent of management

  Original source documents (utility  High — contemporaneous, traceable
  invoices, meter readings)           

  System-generated reports with ITGCs High when controls are effective
  tested                              

  Reconciled management accounts /    Medium-High when reconciliation is
  system exports                      evidenced

  Internal management reports /       Medium — risk of manipulation if no
  spreadsheets                        controls

  Management representations letter   Low alone — corroborating evidence
                                      required

  Oral representations from           Low — must be corroborated, not
  management                          standalone
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

Primary Data Systems — Assessment

-   ESGCaffe platform: assess completeness of data entry, audit trail
      integrity, access controls, and reconciliation to source systems.

-   ERP (SAP/Oracle): typically reliable for energy costs, waste
      disposal costs, and HR headcount. Extract transaction-level data
      and trace to invoices.

-   HRIS (Workday/SuccessFactors/PeopleStrong): primary source for
      workforce metrics — headcount, gender distribution, training
      hours, attrition.

-   Energy Management System (EMS): if available, compare to utility
      billing data. Flag unexplained variances.

-   Spreadsheets: inherently higher risk. Assess: version control,
      formula integrity, access restrictions, independent review.

Site Visits — Decision Framework

-   Reasonable assurance: physical site visits are typically required
      for all material locations (>10-15% of total emissions/headcount
      individually, or top sites collectively covering 70-80% of
      population).

-   Limited assurance: site visits are not always required but should be
      performed for high-risk or high-materiality sites.

-   Virtual walkthroughs (video call with document sharing) are
      acceptable for: lower-risk sites, international locations where
      travel is impractical, pandemic or access restrictions.

-   Site visit objectives: observe metering/measurement equipment,
      review physical documents, interview data owners, assess process
      and controls in operation.

  ⚠️ India: manufacturing sites with high emissions or H&S incidents
  should always have a physical visit under reasonable assurance,
  regardless of materiality.

Step 5 — Environmental Procedures

GHG Emissions — Scope 1

Scope 1: direct emissions from sources owned or controlled by the
organisation — combustion of fuel in boilers, furnaces, vehicles;
industrial processes; on-site power generation; refrigerant leaks.

-   Objective: Verify that Scope 1 emissions are complete, accurately
      calculated, and consistent with stated methodology and GHG
      Protocol Corporate Standard.

-   Obtain complete list of Scope 1 sources from management. Assess
      completeness against operational data (site list, equipment
      register, vehicle fleet register).

-   Trace fuel consumption quantities (litres/GJ) to fuel purchase
      invoices, fuel stock records, or meter readings. For each major
      source, select a sample and agree quantities to primary documents.

-   Verify emission factors: confirm source (IPCC AR5/AR6, GHG Protocol
      default, national data), vintage year, and GWP values used.
      Recalculate emissions for sampled sources.

-   For process emissions (e.g., calcination in cement): verify the
      stoichiometric calculation methodology and the input quantities
      (e.g., limestone consumed).

-   For fugitive emissions (refrigerant leaks): verify equipment
      inventory and leak rates against service records and F-gas logs.

-   Agree total Scope 1 to the reported figure. Investigate any
      variance.

GHG Emissions — Scope 2

-   Objective: Verify accuracy of location-based and/or market-based
      Scope 2 emissions.

-   Obtain electricity consumption data. Trace to electricity bills,
      smart meter readings, or utility statements. Reconcile billed
      consumption to reported consumption.

-   Location-based: verify the grid emission factor used. For India:
      confirm CEA published factor for the applicable year and
      state/region. Recalculate.

-   Market-based: verify EAC/REC documentation, PPA agreements,
      supplier-specific emission factors (with guarantee of origin).
      Assess if residual mix factor is required.

-   For self-generated renewable energy: verify generation metering data
      and confirm it is excluded from purchased electricity.

GHG Emissions — Scope 3

-   Objective: Verify completeness of categories included and accuracy
      of material categories.

-   Review the entity's Scope 3 inventory against all 15 GHG Protocol
      Scope 3 categories. Assess whether material categories are
      included or disclosure of exclusion is made.

-   For Category 1 (Purchased Goods & Services): verify spend-based or
      supplier-specific methodology, emission factors, and spend data
      source.

-   For Category 3 (Fuel & Energy Activities): verify the upstream
      emissions factors.

-   For Category 11 (Use of Sold Products): verify the product usage
      assumptions and customer emission factors.

-   For Category 15 (Investments): verify the methodology for GHG
      attributable to investment portfolio.

  📌 India BRSR: Scope 3 is encouraged but not mandatory for all
  companies. However, BRSR Core requires disclosure of Scope 3 where
  quantified. The assurance scope should clarify which Scope 3
  categories are included.

Energy Consumption

-   Objective: Verify total energy consumed (fuel + electricity + heat)
      and renewable energy share.

-   Obtain energy consumption schedule broken down by source (grid
      electricity, diesel, petrol, LPG, coal, biomass, solar, wind,
      etc.).

-   Trace each major source to primary records: electricity bills, fuel
      invoices, meter readings, on-site generation logs.

-   Verify caloric values / conversion factors used (GJ/kWh
      conversions). Confirm against GHG Protocol or IEA conversion
      tables.

-   Verify renewable energy claims: confirm RECs/EACs for purchased
      renewables; verify generation metering for on-site renewables.

-   Recalculate energy intensity metrics (per turnover, per unit of
      output).

Water

-   Objective: Verify total water withdrawal, consumption, and
      disclosure of operations in water-stressed areas.

-   Obtain water withdrawal schedule by source (municipal, groundwater,
      surface water, rainwater harvesting, third-party).

-   Trace to utility bills, bore-well log books, water purchase
      invoices, or flow meter readings.

-   Verify identification of water-stressed areas: confirm WRI Aqueduct
      or similar tool was used; cross-reference facility locations with
      high-stress designation.

-   Verify water intensity calculations.

Waste

-   Objective: Verify waste generation quantities and disposal routes.

-   Obtain waste manifest register / waste tracking schedule.

-   For hazardous waste: trace disposal quantities to licensed disposal
      contractor receipts and Form 3/Form 4 records (under Hazardous
      Waste Rules, India).

-   For non-hazardous waste: trace to recycler/disposal contractor
      receipts, internal weighbridge records.

-   Verify that waste intensity metrics are correctly calculated.

Step 6 — Social Procedures

Workforce & Employment

-   Objective: Verify accuracy of headcount, employment type, gender
      distribution, and attrition.

-   Obtain headcount schedule (by gender, employment type, level) as at
      the reporting date. Trace to HRIS system extract.

-   For a sample of employees, agree HRIS record to employment contract
      and payroll record.

-   Verify new hires and separations during the period. Trace to HR
      joining/exit records.

-   Recalculate attrition rate. Agree total headcount to payroll
      records.

-   Verify alignment of permanent vs contract worker count with BRSR
      definitions.

Health & Safety

-   Objective: Verify LTIFR, fatality count, recordable injuries, and
      safety training hours.

-   Obtain incident register / EHS records for the full year.

-   For LTIFR: verify the number of lost time injuries and total hours
      worked. Trace injuries to incident reports, medical certificates,
      and workers' compensation records. Trace hours worked to
      payroll/timesheet records.

-   For fatalities: 100% testing. Verify every fatality against incident
      investigation reports, police/labour authority records, and board
      notifications.

-   For near misses (if disclosed): verify process for recording and
      assess completeness of reporting.

-   Safety training: trace reported hours to training attendance
      records, LMS system export. For a sample, agree to attendance
      sheets.

  ⚠️ India: Section 22 of POSH Act, Factories Act compliance, and ESIC
  records are all relevant supporting evidence for H&S disclosures.
  Fatality and serious injury reporting to labour authorities is
  mandatory — cross-reference disclosed data to statutory filings.

DEI / Gender

-   Verify % women in workforce and management: trace to HRIS.
      Recalculate percentages.

-   Verify gender pay gap: obtain pay data by grade/band and gender.
      Assess calculation methodology. Confirm consistency with BRSR
      disclosure method.

-   For diversity commitments: assess whether stated policies and
      programmes are documented and in operation.

Training & Development

-   Trace training hours to LMS (Learning Management System) records or
      physical attendance registers.

-   Verify average training hours per employee by recalculation.

-   For ESG/sustainability training specifically: confirm training
      content and coverage.

Human Rights & Supply Chain

-   Review human rights policy (existence, board approval, date, scope).

-   Assess due diligence process: review supply chain risk assessment
      documentation, supplier questionnaires, and audit findings.

-   Verify supplier coverage: % of suppliers assessed. Trace to
      procurement records.

-   Review any remediation cases: assess completeness and whether
      outcomes are documented.

CSR / Community

-   Agree CSR spend to Board CSR Committee minutes, CSR utilisation
      report (as per Companies Act 2013, Section 135 for India), and
      bank payment records.

-   Verify CSR spend vs statutory obligation (2% of average net profit
      calculation).

-   Verify beneficiary counts and programme descriptions against project
      reports.

Step 7 — Governance Procedures

Board Composition

-   Verify board composition (total directors, independent directors,
      women directors) against company secretarial records and RoC
      filings (MCA portal for India).

-   Verify % independent directors. Confirm against Listing Obligations
      and Disclosure Requirements (LODR) for Indian listed companies.

-   Verify tenure of each director and flag long-tenure non-independent
      directors.

-   Verify ESG committee / sustainability committee existence: obtain
      committee terms of reference, board resolution approving
      establishment.

Anti-Bribery & Corruption

-   Verify existence, scope, and board approval date of
      anti-bribery/anti-corruption policy.

-   Verify training coverage: % of employees trained on ABC policy.
      Trace to training records.

-   Verify whistleblower mechanism: confirm Vigil
      Mechanism/Whistleblower Policy is in place (mandatory for listed
      companies in India under Companies Act 2013 and SEBI LODR).

-   Review whistleblower reports received during the year and confirm
      disposition process.

Compliance

-   Verify ESG-related fines and sanctions: obtain legal register and
      agree to disclosed amounts. Confirm no undisclosed material
      penalties.

-   Verify regulatory compliance disclosures: cross-reference to
      statutory filings, MCA records, SEBI exchange filings, PCB consent
      orders.

ESG Policies

-   For each disclosed policy (environmental, human rights, DEI, data
      privacy, health & safety), verify: existence, board approval,
      date, and whether it has been communicated to relevant employees.

-   Assess whether stated policy commitments are reflected in
      operational procedures.

Reporting Transparency

-   Identify any restatements of prior year data. Verify that
      restatements are properly disclosed with explanation.

-   Verify methodology changes: confirm any change from prior year
      methods is disclosed and the impact quantified.

-   Verify that omissions (where a KPI is not reported) include a reason
      for omission.

Step 8 — Controls vs Substantive Work

Overview

The balance between controls testing and substantive testing depends on:
the assurance level, the maturity of the entity's ESG data environment,
the effectiveness of controls (if tested), and cost-efficiency
considerations.

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Approach                            When to Use

  Heavy controls reliance             Reasonable assurance + mature ESG
                                      platform (ESGCaffe) + strong
                                      ITGCs + effective approvals
                                      workflow. Reduces substantive
                                      sample sizes.

  Balanced                            Reasonable assurance + moderate
                                      controls + mix of automated and
                                      manual processes.

  Primarily substantive               Limited assurance (controls
                                      assessment is inquiry-based only)
                                      OR reasonable assurance where
                                      controls have failed or are
                                      immature.

  Controls only where mandated        Specific requirements in the
                                      standard (e.g., ISAE 3000 requires
                                      assessment of design effectiveness
                                      under reasonable assurance).
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

Controls Assessment — Design

-   Walk through each major data stream (GHG calculation, energy
      metering, HRIS extraction) to document the control activities at
      each step.

-   Assess whether each control is suitably designed to prevent or
      detect material misstatement.

-   Document the assessment in a process flowchart or narrative memo.

-   Use COSO Internal Control – Integrated Framework as a reference for
      control design principles.

Controls Assessment — Operating Effectiveness

-   Select a sample of instances where the control should have operated
      during the year.

-   Inspect evidence that the control operated as designed (e.g.,
      dual-authorisation log, system approval timestamp, review sign-off
      on report).

-   Evaluate deviation rate. Reassess risk and adjust substantive
      procedures if deviations exceed tolerable rate.

ITGCs — ESGCaffe / Digital Platform Context

-   Access controls: verify that user access rights to ESGCaffe are
      appropriate (principle of least privilege). Confirm that data
      entry, review, and approval roles are segregated.

-   Change management: verify that changes to calculation logic,
      emission factors, or system configurations are subject to a
      controlled change process.

-   Data integrity: confirm that data once submitted cannot be altered
      without leaving an audit trail. Verify audit log completeness.

-   Backup and availability: confirm that system data is backed up and
      recoverable.

Substantive Procedures

-   Recalculation: re-perform the entity's calculations (GHG totals,
      intensity metrics, workforce ratios) using the raw data and stated
      methodology.

-   Vouching: trace a sample of reported figures back to source
      documents (invoices, meter readings, HR records).

-   Reconciliation: reconcile reported ESG data to source systems
      (ESGCaffe to ERP, EMS to utility billing) and investigate
      variances.

-   Analytical procedures: compare current year to prior year; compare
      to industry benchmarks; compare facility-level data to corporate
      totals.

Step 9 — Sampling & Coverage

Population Definition

-   Define the population for each metric (e.g., total electricity
      consumption sites, total employees).

-   Stratify where appropriate: by size (large vs small sites), by risk
      (high-risk vs routine), by geography.

Sampling Approaches

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Approach                            Description & When to Use

  Risk-based judgemental              Auditor selects items based on risk
                                      characteristics. Used under limited
                                      assurance for all metrics; under
                                      reasonable assurance for high-risk
                                      items not covered by statistical
                                      sampling.

  Statistical random sampling         Mathematically selected sample.
                                      Provides the ability to project
                                      findings to the population. Used
                                      under reasonable assurance for
                                      large populations of transactions.

  Full population (census)            100% testing. Used when population
                                      is small (<10-20 items), for
                                      fatalities/serious incidents
                                      (always 100%), for material
                                      individual transactions.

  Stratified sampling                 Population divided into strata;
                                      sample selected from each stratum
                                      based on relative size/risk.
                                      Efficient for heterogeneous
                                      populations.
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

Coverage Thresholds — Recommended Benchmarks

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  KPI Category                        Recommended Coverage (Reasonable
                                      Assurance)

  Scope 1 GHG                         ≥80% of total Scope 1 emissions by
                                      source

  Scope 2 GHG                         ≥90% of purchased electricity by
                                      location

  Scope 3 (material categories)       ≥75% of estimated activity data for
                                      each material category

  Energy consumption                  ≥75% of total energy by source and
                                      site

  Water withdrawal                    ≥70% of total water withdrawal by
                                      source

  Total headcount                     ≥80% of total headcount (verify via
                                      HRIS system-level test)

  H&S — fatalities                    100% — all fatalities must be
                                      verified

  H&S — LTIFR                         ≥80% of total workforce hours
                                      worked

  Training hours                      ≥70% of total training hours (or
                                      full population if LMS available)

  CSR spend                           ≥85% of total CSR spend by
                                      programme

  Board/governance                    100% of board members for
                                      composition metrics
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

  📌 Under limited assurance, these coverage floors can be reduced —
  typically 50-60% for emissions and 60-70% for workforce metrics. The
  key is to be able to form a reasonable basis for the negative
  conclusion.

Sample Size Determination

-   Statistical sampling: use confidence level (typically 90-95% for
      reasonable, 80-90% for limited), expected error rate, and
      tolerable error rate to calculate sample size via standard audit
      sampling tables or software.

-   Judgemental sampling: document the rationale for the sample
      selected, including risk factors that influenced selection.

-   Minimum items: regardless of statistical calculation, always test at
      least 15-20 items for a population to draw meaningful conclusions.

Assurance Report — Required Sections

-   Title: 'Independent Assurance Report of [Firm Name] to [Addressee]'

-   Addressee: Board of Directors (or Audit/ESG Committee) and/or
      shareholders

-   Our Conclusion / Our Opinion: the conclusion paragraph — must use
      the correct wording for the assurance level

-   Basis for Conclusion: summary of work performed — more detailed
      under limited assurance to justify the scope

-   Management's Responsibility: preparation of disclosures, internal
      controls, and choice of criteria

-   Practitioner's Responsibility: our responsibility is to express a
      conclusion based on our procedures

-   Standard Reference: we conducted our engagement in accordance with
      [ISAE 3000/3410/ISSA 5000 etc.]

-   Inherent Limitations: nature of sustainability information —
      estimation, judgement, selective nature of procedures

-   Independence and Ethics: we are independent of [Company] in
      accordance with [applicable code of ethics]

-   SEBI Registration (India): registration number of the assurance
      provider

-   Signature: engagement partner's name, professional designation, firm
      name

-   Date: date of the assurance report (typically close to or coinciding
      with the sustainability report publication date)

-   Address: firm's office address

Conclusion Wording — Standard Templates

  📌 LIMITED ASSURANCE (ISAE 3000): 'Based on our procedures and the
  evidence we have obtained, nothing has come to our attention that
  causes us to believe that the [subject matter] of [Company Name] for
  the year ended [date], is not prepared, in all material respects, in
  accordance with the [criteria] as described in [section of report].'

  📌 REASONABLE ASSURANCE (ISAE 3000): 'In our opinion, the [subject
  matter] of [Company Name] for the year ended [date] is prepared, in
  all material respects, in accordance with the [criteria] as described
  in [section of report].'

  📌 LIMITED ASSURANCE (ISSA 5000): 'Based on the procedures we
  performed and the evidence we obtained, nothing has come to our
  attention that causes us to believe that the sustainability statement
  of [Company] for [period] does not give a true and fair view in
  accordance with [applicable framework].'

Quality Control

-   Engagement Quality Control Review (EQCR/EQR): an independent review
      by a senior practitioner not involved in the engagement. Typically
      required for: listed companies, high-risk engagements, new
      engagements. Must be completed before the report is issued.

-   Second partner review: additional review layer at many firms for
      BRSR Core engagements.

-   Independence re-confirmation: the engagement partner must re-confirm
      independence immediately before issuing the report.

-   Cold review: a reviewer with no involvement reads the workpapers and
      report to identify gaps.

-   File completion: all workpapers must be finalised and filed within
      60 days of the report date (many firms use 45 days as the
      standard).

  SECTION 4 — Materiality: Detailed Reference

4.1 Materiality Thresholds by KPI Type

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  KPI Category                        Typical Quantitative Materiality

  GHG Scope 1 & 2 (tCO₂e)             5% of total Scope 1+2 emissions;
                                      consider lower (3%) for BRSR Core
                                      KPIs

  GHG Scope 3 (tCO₂e)                 5-10% by category — higher
                                      tolerable due to inherent
                                      estimation uncertainty

  Energy (GJ or MWh)                  5% of total energy consumption

  Water (ML)                          5% of total water withdrawal or
                                      consumption

  Waste (tonnes)                      5-10% of total waste generated by
                                      category

  Headcount                           1-2% of total workforce (low
                                      threshold — individual count errors
                                      are significant)

  LTIFR                               5% — but all fatalities are always
                                      material regardless of threshold

  Gender metrics (%)                  1-2 percentage points — small %
                                      changes in diversity ratios are
                                      material to users

  CSR spend (₹)                       5% of total CSR spend; or 2% of net
                                      profit (the statutory obligation)
                                      if smaller

  Board composition (%)               1 director — always material
                                      (binary)
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

4.2 Qualitative Materiality Factors

-   Regulatory significance: KPIs specifically required by SEBI BRSR
      Core are always material regardless of size.

-   First-year disclosure: any metric being disclosed for the first time
      carries elevated inherent risk.

-   Public target: if the company has made a public commitment (e.g.,
      net zero by 2040, 40% women in leadership by 2027), metrics
      related to that target have elevated qualitative materiality.

-   Investor/analyst focus: metrics that are specifically questioned by
      institutional investors, ESG ratings agencies, or analyst reports.

-   Controversy: if there has been a public incident (spill, fatality,
      governance failure), related metrics carry elevated qualitative
      materiality.

-   Trend reversal: a metric that has worsened significantly from prior
      year may be material even if the absolute change is below the
      quantitative threshold.

4.3 Double Materiality (CSRD / ISSA 5000 Context)

Double materiality requires the assurance practitioner to consider two
perspectives:

-   Financial materiality (inside-out): how does the sustainability
      topic affect the company's financial performance, position, and
      cash flows? Relevant for ISSB/TCFD-aligned disclosures.

-   Impact materiality (outside-in): what impact does the company have
      on people and the environment? Relevant for GRI and CSRD/ESRS
      disclosures.

Both perspectives are relevant for ISSA 5000 and CSRD engagements. ISAE
3000 is flexible enough to accommodate both, though it does not
explicitly mandate the double materiality lens.

  SECTION 5 — India BRSR: Deep Dive for Assurance Practitioners

5.1 BRSR Structure — What the Auditor Must Know

BRSR has three sections:

-   Section A: General Disclosures — company overview, business
      activities, CSR details, transparency disclosures.

-   Section B: Management & Process Disclosures — policies, governance
      structures, targets, supply chain.

-   Section C: Principle-wise Performance — nine NGRBC (National
      Guidelines for Responsible Business Conduct) principles with
      quantitative and qualitative disclosures.

BRSR Core is a separate subset of principally quantitative KPIs within
Section C that require third-party assurance.

5.2 BRSR Core KPIs — Assurance Requirements

5.3 SEBI Regulatory Requirements for Assurance

-   Mandatory assurance: Top 150 companies (by market cap) from FY
      2023-24. Phased rollout to Top 1,000 by approximately FY 2026-27.

-   Assurance provider must be SEBI-registered. Include registration
      number in report.

-   Independence: the assurance provider cannot be the same entity that
      has provided significant consulting, advisory, or data management
      services to the company in the same year in a way that impairs
      independence.

-   The assurance report must be included in the Annual Report (embedded
      or appended) alongside the BRSR.

-   SEBI expects: explicit statement of the standard used, the level of
      assurance, the specific KPIs covered, and any limitations.

5.4 Common Errors in BRSR Assurance — India

  ⚠️ Using outdated CEA grid emission factors (e.g., using 2019 factor
  in FY 2024 reporting).

  ⚠️ Incorrect GHG intensity denominator — mixing standalone turnover
  with consolidated turnover, or inconsistent year-on-year denominator
  definition.

  ⚠️ Under-counting Scope 3 — omitting major categories (e.g., Category
  1 purchased goods, Category 11 product use) without disclosure.

  ⚠️ H&S under-reporting — contractor incidents excluded from LTIFR
  calculation without disclosure; fatality count differs from labour
  authority filings.

  ⚠️ Gender pay gap methodology not disclosed — different results
  depending on mean/median, grade bands, inclusions of senior
  management.

  ⚠️ SEBI registration number missing from assurance report — renders
  the assurance non-compliant for BRSR Core purposes.

  ⚠️ Assurance scope does not match BRSR filing boundary — subsidiaries
  excluded from assurance that are included in the BRSR.

6.2 Choosing the Right Standard — Decision Tree

Use this logic:

-   Is the engagement purely for GHG emissions (CDP, SBTi, standalone
      carbon inventory)? → ISAE 3410

-   Is the client a listed Indian company needing BRSR Core assurance? →
      ISAE 3000 (with ICAI Technical Guide as supplementary reference)

-   Is the client a GRI reporter with strong stakeholder engagement
      focus? → AA1000AS v3 (Type 2)

-   Is the client cross-listed in the EU or preparing for CSRD? → ISSA
      5000

-   Is the practitioner an ICAI-registered CA in India? → ISAE 3000 +
      ICAI Technical Guide

-   Does the firm have its own methodology? → Firm-specific, documented
      to be consistent with ISAE 3000 principles

7.2 Management Representation Letter — Key Points

A management representation letter (MRL) must be obtained from senior
management (typically CEO and/or CFO) and the ESG/Sustainability Head
before the assurance report is issued. Key representations include:

-   All known data errors, misstatements, or omissions have been
      disclosed to the assurance provider.

-   The disclosures have been prepared in accordance with the stated
      criteria and reporting framework.

-   All evidence requested has been provided; no significant information
      has been withheld.

-   Management is responsible for the design and operation of internal
      controls over ESG reporting.

-   There are no unresolved matters that would affect the assurance
      conclusion.

-   Any subsequent events between the data reporting date and the
      assurance report date that could materially affect the disclosures
      have been disclosed.

  ⚠️ The MRL reduces but does NOT eliminate the need for independent
  evidence. Under ISAE 3000, the MRL is corroborating evidence only — it
  cannot be the sole basis for a conclusion on any specific assertion.

  SECTION 8 — AI Assistant Knowledge Base: Q&A Reference

This section provides the knowledge base that powers the ESGCaffe
AssuranceAI assistant. For each common auditor question, the correct
authoritative answer is documented.

Step 1 — Standard & Scope Q&A

Q: What KPIs must be covered under BRSR Core mandatory assurance?

BRSR Core includes: Scope 1 GHG (tCO₂e), Scope 2 GHG (tCO₂e), GHG
intensity (per ₹ turnover and per unit output), total energy (GJ), %
renewable energy, energy intensity, water withdrawal (ML), water
intensity, LTIFR, fatalities, % women in workforce, % women in
management, gender pay gap, board composition (% women, % independent),
ESG committee existence, CSR spend vs obligation, supply chain ESG
assessment coverage. See SEBI circular for the current definitive list.

Q: Can I narrow the entity boundary vs the BRSR filing?

No — the assurance scope must match the BRSR filing boundary. Any
narrowing must be disclosed explicitly in the assurance report as a
scope limitation. SEBI may not recognise the assurance as satisfying the
BRSR Core requirement if there is an undisclosed boundary mismatch.

Step 2 — Assurance Level Q&A

Q: Can I provide limited assurance on all BRSR Core KPIs?

Yes, limited assurance is currently acceptable for BRSR Core. However,
SEBI has signalled an expectation that companies will progress toward
reasonable assurance on the most material KPIs (GHG, energy, H&S) over
time. Large caps are increasingly expected to have reasonable assurance
on at least the BRSR Core emissions and safety metrics within 2-4 years
of BRSR adoption.

Q: What is the correct conclusion wording for limited assurance under
ISAE 3000 in India?

'Based on our procedures and the evidence we have obtained, nothing has
come to our attention that causes us to believe that the BRSR Core
disclosures of [Company Name] for the financial year ended 31 March 20XX
are not prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with the BRSR
disclosure requirements issued by SEBI vide circular [number].'

Step 3 — Materiality Q&A

Q: What percentage should I use as overall materiality for GHG?

5% of total Scope 1+2 emissions is the most commonly used quantitative
threshold for GHG assurance. For BRSR Core engagements, some
practitioners use 3% to reflect the elevated regulatory significance.
For Scope 3, 5-10% per material category is typical given higher
inherent estimation uncertainty. Always document the rationale for the
threshold chosen.

Q: Is a fatality always material regardless of my materiality threshold?

Yes. Any fatality is qualitatively material regardless of its size or
the quantitative threshold. Fatalities are subject to 100% testing. A
conclusion cannot be issued if any fatality has not been fully verified
and investigated.

Step 4 — Evidence Q&A

Q: Can I rely on ESGCaffe platform data as primary evidence?

Yes, but only if ITGCs have been assessed and found effective. ESGCaffe
data is reliable when: access controls are appropriate, the audit trail
is complete and unaltered, data entry and approval workflows are
operating effectively, and the system data has been reconciled to source
records. If ITGCs have not been tested, or if they are found to be
ineffective, substantive testing must extend to underlying source
documents for the affected data streams.

Q: What India-specific sources do I need for Scope 2 verification?

Central Electricity Authority (CEA) publication 'CO2 Baseline Database
for the Indian Power Sector' — verify the version and reference year.
This is updated annually and the correct version for the reporting year
must be used. Do not use IEA India grid factor or global averages as a
substitute unless the CEA factor is unavailable for a specific
state/region.

Step 5 — Environmental Q&A

Q: Which GHG Protocol documents are the primary criteria?

(1) GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (the
'Corporate Standard') — primary reference for Scope 1 and 2. (2) GHG
Protocol Scope 2 Guidance — for market-based Scope 2. (3) GHG Protocol
Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Standard — for Scope 3. (4) GHG Protocol
Calculation Tools — for specific sector calculations. In India, these
are complemented by CEA grid emission factors.

Q: What GWP values should be used for India BRSR?

BRSR SEBI guidance references IPCC AR5 GWP values (CH4 = 28, N2O = 265)
as the primary reference. Some companies use AR4 values (CH4 = 25, N2O =
298) for continuity with prior years. The assurance practitioner must
verify that: (a) the GWP source is disclosed, (b) the values are
correctly applied, and (c) any change from prior year is disclosed with
the impact quantified.

Step 9 — Sampling Q&A

Q: What is the minimum coverage for reasonable assurance on Scope 1?

The commonly used benchmark is ≥80% of total Scope 1 emissions by
source. This means the sampled sources, when aggregated, must account
for at least 80% of the total reported Scope 1 figure. High-emission
sources are typically selected with certainty (100%) and remaining
coverage is met through risk-stratified sampling of smaller sources.

Q: Can I use 100% testing instead of sampling for small populations?

Yes — for small populations (typically fewer than 15-20 items), full
population testing is appropriate and removes sampling risk entirely.
This is especially recommended for: fatalities (always 100%), board
composition (always 100%), and small companies with fewer than 5
production facilities.

Step 10 — Reporting Q&A

Q: Is EQCR mandatory for BRSR assurance?

Under ISAE 3000, EQCR (Engagement Quality Control Review) is required
for listed entities and high-risk engagements. For BRSR Core assurance
(which applies to listed companies by definition), EQCR should be
treated as mandatory. The EQCR reviewer must be a qualified, independent
practitioner not involved in the engagement, who reviews the workpapers
and conclusion before the report is issued.

Q: What must the assurance report include for SEBI to recognise it?

SEBI requires: (1) identification of the assurance provider's SEBI
registration number; (2) explicit statement of the standard used (e.g.,
ISAE 3000 Revised); (3) the level of assurance (limited or reasonable);
(4) the specific KPIs or subject matter covered; (5) the criteria used;
(6) any scope limitations or exclusions; (7) the conclusion in the
correct form (negative for limited, positive for reasonable); (8) the
date, firm name, practitioner signature, and address.
